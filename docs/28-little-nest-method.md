# Purpose

The Little Nest Method defines how we teach.

It is not a curriculum.

It is not a lesson plan.

It does not replace Department of Education standards.

Instead, it explains the teaching approach that creates the Little Nest experience.

Regardless of the activity being taught, the principles within this document should guide every teacher, every classroom, and every interaction with children and families.

---

# Our Goal

Our goal is not simply to teach children.

Our goal is to help children love learning.

Knowledge is important.

Confidence is important.

Curiosity is important.

Kindness is important.

Character is important.

Every lesson should help children grow as people, not simply increase what they know.

---

# Teach the Child Before the Lesson

Every child enters the classroom differently.

Some arrive excited.

Some arrive quietly.

Some arrive cautiously.

Some arrive full of energy.

The first responsibility of a teacher is not to begin the lesson.

It is to understand the child.

When children feel safe, welcomed, and understood, they become ready to learn.

---

# Build Trust First

Learning grows from trust.

Children who trust their teachers are more willing to:

Ask questions.

Try something new.

Make mistakes.

Help others.

Express themselves.

Trust is the foundation upon which every lesson is built.

---

# Invite, Never Force

Children should never feel pressured to participate.

Learning is an invitation.

Teachers encourage.

Support.

Model.

Celebrate.

When a child is not ready today, we patiently prepare them for tomorrow.

Every child develops at their own pace.

---

# Follow the Child

Children learn differently.

Some learn by watching.

Some by talking.

Some by moving.

Some by creating.

Great teachers observe first.

Then they adapt.

The lesson should serve the child.

The child should never be forced to serve the lesson.

---

# Learning Through Play

Play is one of the most powerful forms of learning.

Children naturally explore.

Experiment.

Imagine.

Collaborate.

Solve problems.

Play should be purposeful, joyful, and meaningful.

Children should leave class remembering how they felt, not just what they completed.

---

# Celebrate Progress

Progress deserves recognition.

Not because children reached perfection.

Because they tried.

Every small success builds confidence.

Confidence encourages the next attempt.

Celebration creates momentum.

---

# Notice the Invisible Moments

Some of the most important learning cannot be measured.

A first smile.

A first friendship.

Helping another child.

Waiting patiently.

Showing kindness.

Trying again after failing.

Teachers should notice these moments.

Families treasure them.

---

# Every Child Has a Story

Every class becomes another chapter.

Teachers are helping write that story.

Parents experience that story through Story Reel.

Memory Nest preserves that story.

Every lesson contributes to a child's lifelong journey.

---

# Partner With Parents

Parents remain a child's most important teachers.

The Little Nest strengthens that relationship.

Teachers should provide parents with meaningful moments they can talk about at home.

The conversation after school is part of the learning experience.

---

# Tita Mayumi and Pico

Tita Mayumi represents the teacher every child deserves.

Patient.

Kind.

Wise.

Encouraging.

Pico represents every child.

Curious.

Learning.

Sometimes making mistakes.

Always growing.

Children learn alongside Pico.

Teachers guide with the same patience as Tita Mayumi.

---

# Consistency Across Every Little Nest

Every teacher has their own personality.

Every classroom has its own character.

Every community is unique.

But every child should experience:

Safety.

Encouragement.

Joy.

Curiosity.

Belonging.

Every parent should experience:

Trust.

Connection.

Confidence.

Pride.

The Little Nest feeling should remain consistent wherever a family visits.

---

# Measure Success Differently

Success is not measured by comparison.

It is not measured only by worksheets completed.

It is measured by growth.

Did the child become more confident?

Did they become more curious?

Did they try?

Did they smile?

Did they feel proud?

Those questions matter just as much as academic progress.

# Final Principle

Every decision inside The Little Nest should answer one simple question:

**"Will this help a child become a little better today than they were yesterday?"**

If the answer is yes...

We are moving in the right direction.
